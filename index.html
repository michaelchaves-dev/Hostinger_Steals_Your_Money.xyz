Receipt #4 — The design is the trap

These are the patterns I documented in my own account, repeatedly:

  • Buried options. The setting you actually want sits layers deep, while the more expensive plan is the big shiny default button. My experience
  • Mis-assigned websites. Newly published sites get attached to the wrong domain in the publish flow — and fixing their routing mistake becomes your problem. My experience
  • The refund hourglass. 24–48 hours to claim a refund; 1–2 days for their own sites to fully propagate. By the time you can see the product is broken, the refund window has closed. My experience
  • Instant when caught, policy when not. One documented case: confronted with the evidence, support refunded about $100 on the spot. Every other time: “company policy.” My experience

I keep an archive of screenshots documenting these patterns. This page will grow as the archive is organized and published.

Before you buy hosting — anywhere

  • Screenshot every plan-selection screen. The default button is not your friend.
  • Check your site from a stranger’s browser within the refund window — not after.
  • Verify each published site is attached to the domain you actually chose.
  • Calendar every renewal and suspension notice the day it arrives.
  • Keep your registrar and your hosting separable, so one company can’t hold both hostage.
